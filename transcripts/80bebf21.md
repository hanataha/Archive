It was a Tuesday evening in late April, and the cherry blossoms outside had already begun to fade, their pink petals scattered across the narrow streets like forgotten confetti.

“Finally… home,” he muttered to himself, a small smile tugging at his lips.

He kicked off his sneakers at the genkan, not bothering to line them up perfectly—there was no one here to scold him anyway. His parents were back in Fukuoka, a good ten-hour shinkansen ride away. They called every Sunday like clockwork, asking if he was eating properly and if he’d made any “nice friends” at university. Eon always gave the same vague, reassuring answers: “Yeah, I’m fine. Classes are okay. Made some friends in the club.” He never mentioned how much he preferred coming back to this empty apartment over hanging out late with those friends.

The apartment was tiny by any standard—about 20 square meters—but to Eon, it was his personal kingdom. One room served as living room, bedroom, study, and dining area all at once. The walls were lined with cheap metal shelves he’d assembled himself from IKEA, now groaning under the weight of his treasures: rows upon rows of anime figures still in their boxes, a carefully curated manga collection organized by genre and release year, and several Gunpla models in various stages of completion. In the corner stood his pride and joy: a 27-inch curved monitor mounted on a sturdy desk, flanked by RGB keyboard and mouse that glowed softly in the dim light.

Eon dropped his backpack on the floor and immediately changed into his “home uniform”—a faded black hoodie with a chibi print of Rem from Re:Zero on the front, paired with loose gray sweatpants. No need for pants that actually fit properly when you lived alone. He stretched his arms above his head, hearing a few satisfying pops from his spine after a long day of lectures.

“Alright… let’s get the vibe right,” he said aloud to the empty room.

He walked over to his small speaker and connected his phone. A few taps later, the opening theme of his current favorite isekai anime began playing at a comfortable volume. The upbeat yet slightly melancholic melody filled the room. Eon hummed along as he moved to the kitchenette, which was basically a sink, a mini-fridge, and a single induction stove. He pulled out a cup of instant ramen—tonkotsu flavor, his go-to—and started boiling water in the electric kettle.

While waiting, he glanced at the calendar stuck on the wall. Midterms were coming up in two weeks, but he wasn’t too worried. His grades were decent enough; he wasn’t the top student in his literature department, but he wasn’t failing either. University life in Tokyo was exactly what he had imagined—crowded trains, overwhelming campus events, and endless opportunities to disappear into the crowd. He attended his classes, took notes, occasionally joined group projects, and then… retreated.

His friends from the anime and game club—mostly fellow otakus like him—would sometimes invite him out for karaoke or late-night convenience store runs. Eon liked them well enough. There was Takashi, the loud one who always talked about the latest gacha game meta, and Yumi, the girl who cosplayed as her favorite characters and somehow made even the most niche references sound cool. They were good company. But after a few hours, Eon would start feeling that familiar itch. The need to be alone. To recharge in his own space, on his own terms.

He didn’t hate people. He just loved his self-time more.

The kettle clicked off. Eon poured the hot water into the ramen cup, covered it with the lid, and set a five-minute timer on his phone. While it steeped, he powered on his PC. The fans whirred to life with a low, soothing hum. His desktop background was a beautiful illustrated wallpaper of a lone adventurer standing on a cliff overlooking a fantasy kingdom at sunset—a scene from the game he’d been sinking hundreds of hours into lately: Eternal Realms, an open-world MMORPG with deep lore, beautiful graphics, and a surprisingly active Japanese server.

Eon logged in. His character, a silver-haired dark elf mage named “VoidWalker_Eon,” materialized on screen. He had spent an embarrassing number of hours customizing every detail—the exact shade of his cloak, the enchantments on his staff, even the idle animations. In Eternal Realms, he was confident, powerful, and occasionally flirted with NPC tavern girls in the game’s surprisingly well-written dialogue trees. In real life… not so much.

Romance? That was a foreign concept.

Eon had never even held a girl’s hand, let alone gone on a date. In high school back in Fukuoka, he’d been too busy reading light novels during lunch breaks and grinding mobile games to notice the girls around him. When they did talk to him, he’d usually respond with short, awkward sentences and then retreat back into his shell. University in Tokyo was no different. There were plenty of cute girls in his classes and in the club—some even shared his interests—but the idea of actually asking one out made his stomach twist with anxiety.

What would he even say? “Hey, do you want to come over and watch the new season of Slime Tensei while I eat cup noodles?” Yeah, that would go over great.

No. He was perfectly fine like this. Single. Uncomplicated. Free to spend his evenings exactly how he wanted.

The timer on his phone beeped. Eon peeled back the lid of the ramen, inhaling the salty, savory aroma with a contented grin. He carried the cup over to his desk, placed it on a coaster shaped like a Pokeball, and slipped on his gaming headset. The world outside his apartment— the salarymen rushing home, the university clubs hosting mixers, the couples walking hand-in-hand along the Sumida River—faded away.

In here, it was just him, his ramen, his anime OST, and the glowing screen.

He took a big slurp of noodles, the broth warming him from the inside. On screen, VoidWalker_Eon joined a raid party with some guildmates. One of them typed in chat:

[Guild] ShadowBlade99: Yo Eon! You’re late again lol. Grinding solo again?

Eon chuckled and typed back quickly with one hand while holding chopsticks in the other.

[Party] VoidWalker_Eon: Sorry, real life stuff. Ready to carry you guys though.

The raid started. Colorful spell effects exploded across the screen as his dark elf mage unleashed chain lightning and void orbs. Eon’s focus narrowed entirely to the game. For the next few hours, nothing else existed. No midterms. No social obligations. No confusing feelings about girls he occasionally noticed but never approached.

Around 11 PM, after the raid cleared successfully and his guildmates logged off with cheerful goodbyes, Eon leaned back in his chair and stretched again. His ramen cup was empty, the anime playlist on his speaker had moved on to lo-fi remixes of classic openings. The room was bathed in the soft blue and purple glow of his RGB lights.

He opened a new tab on his browser and scrolled through his favorite anime news site, reading spoilers for the upcoming season of a highly anticipated adaptation. Then he checked a few Reddit threads on r/anime and r/gaming, laughing quietly at the memes. One post in particular caught his eye: a fellow otaku complaining about how hard it was to balance university life with their weeb hobbies. The comments were filled with relatable pain.

Eon smiled to himself. He didn’t have that problem. He had perfected the balance.

Or rather… he had chosen his side.

After washing the ramen cup and brushing his teeth, he changed into his sleeping clothes—an oversized t-shirt with a faded print of Attack on Titan’s Survey Corps emblem. He turned off the main lights, leaving only the gentle glow of his PC in sleep mode and a small nightlight shaped like Totoro.

Lying on his futon, which he’d folded out from the closet, Eon stared at the ceiling for a moment. The distant sound of a train rumbling somewhere in the city reached his ears faintly. Somewhere out there, people were probably at izakayas laughing with friends, or on dates under the remaining cherry blossom trees, or studying late in the library.

He felt a tiny, fleeting pang of something—loneliness? Curiosity? He wasn’t sure. But it passed quickly, replaced by the deep, warm satisfaction of a day that had ended exactly the way he liked it.

Tomorrow would be more classes, maybe club activities if he felt like it, then back here. More anime. More gaming. More peaceful solitude.

Eon pulled the blanket up to his chin and closed his eyes, a small, genuine smile on his face.

“Yeah… this is nice,” he whispered to the empty room.

In the quiet of his little Tokyo apartment, surrounded by the things he loved, Eon drifted off to sleep feeling perfectly content with his single, otaku life. No complications. No heartbreaks. Just him, his hobbies, and the endless worlds waiting on his screen.

Little did he know, life in Tokyo had a way of surprising even the most dedicated lone wolves. But for now, on this ordinary spring night, Eon’s small kingdom was exactly the paradise he had always wanted.

The next morning, sunlight filtered through the thin curtains of his apartment. Eon groaned softly as his phone alarm blared the instrumental version of “Gurenge” from Demon Slayer. He slapped the snooze button once, then twice, before finally sitting up with messy bed hair.

“Five more minutes…” he mumbled, but he knew better. If he snoozed again, he’d miss the train and be late for his 9:30 literature seminar.

He dragged himself out of the futon and shuffled to the bathroom. The face looking back at him in the mirror was average—messy black hair that refused to behave, slightly pale skin from spending too much time indoors, and tired eyes that brightened only when discussing plot twists or game builds. He wasn’t ugly, but he wasn’t the type of guy who turned heads either. And that was fine with him.

After a quick shower and throwing on a simple hoodie and jeans, Eon grabbed his backpack. He made sure to pack his portable charger, a volume of the latest manga he was reading, and a small bento he’d prepared the night before—rice, karaage, and some pickled vegetables. Nothing fancy, but it saved him money and the hassle of queuing at the crowded university cafeteria.

Stepping outside, the cool spring air greeted him. Nakano was still relatively quiet at this hour. Salarymen in suits hurried toward the station, high school students in uniforms chatted loudly, and a few elderly residents walked their dogs. Eon blended right in, earphones in, listening to a podcast about upcoming game releases as he walked to the station.

The train was, as usual, packed. He managed to find a spot near the door and pulled out his manga, losing himself in the pages even as bodies pressed around him. This was another reason he loved living alone in Tokyo—no one at home waiting for him to report his day, no pressure to join every social event. He could just… exist.

At university, the day unfolded predictably. Morning seminar on modern Japanese literature (he actually enjoyed this one, especially when they discussed authors who influenced anime and manga), afternoon elective on digital media, and a quick stop at the club room during lunch break.

Takashi waved him over enthusiastically. “Eon! You free this Friday? We’re planning a watch party for the new episode of Reincarnated as a Vending Machine. Yumi’s bringing snacks!”

Eon hesitated for half a second. The idea sounded fun in theory—group reactions, laughing at the ridiculous premise, sharing theories. But then he imagined coming home late, tired, and not having time to grind his dailies in Eternal Realms or finish the manga he was on.

“Uh… maybe,” he replied with a sheepish smile. “I’ll let you know. Got some assignments piling up.”

Takashi nodded understandingly. “No worries, man. You always disappear into your cave after classes anyway. Respect.”

Yumi, who was painting her nails with chibi designs at the table, glanced up and grinned. “If you change your mind, bring that limited edition figure you showed us last time! We can do a mini photoshoot.”

Eon chuckled awkwardly. “Yeah… maybe.”

He liked Yumi. She was energetic, knowledgeable about obscure mecha anime, and surprisingly easy to talk to. But every time she smiled at him like that, a small voice in his head panicked: What if she’s just being nice? What if I say something stupid? Better not risk it.

After club, he attended his last class, took diligent notes, and then made his way back to the station as the sun began to dip lower in the sky.

Back in his apartment by 6:30 PM, Eon repeated his sacred ritual. Shoes off. Hoodie on. Lights dimmed. Anime music playing. This time it was the full soundtrack from Violet Evergarden—calmer, more emotional tones that matched his reflective mood.

He cooked a simple dinner—stir-fried vegetables with rice and a fried egg on top—while watching a YouTube video essay about the evolution of isekai tropes. The food tasted better because he ate it while learning something new about his favorite genre.

After dinner came the best part of the day: uninterrupted gaming time.

He joined a voice chat with a couple of his online guildmates—people he’d never met in real life but felt closer to than most of his university friends. They raided a difficult dungeon, strategized for hours, and celebrated when they finally downed the boss after seventeen attempts.

“Nice clutch heal, Eon!” one of them shouted through the headset.

“Thanks,” Eon replied, grinning ear to ear. In these moments, he felt alive in a way lectures and group projects never made him feel.

When the session ended around midnight, he leaned back, exhausted but happy. He scrolled through Twitter (or X, as it was now called) for anime memes, favorited a few fan arts of his favorite characters, and then decided to treat himself to a late-night snack: a pack of Pocky and a can of coffee milk from the fridge.

Lying in bed again that night, Eon thought about how perfect his routine was. Lectures in the day, weeb paradise at night. No drama. No expectations. Just pure, unfiltered enjoyment of the things he loved.

He didn’t need romance. He didn’t need constant company. He had his figures watching over him from the shelves, his games waiting on the hard drive, and his stories ready to unfold on screen.

Tokyo was a big city full of possibilities, but for Eon, the best possibility was right here in his tiny apartment—being a happy, single, dedicated otaku and gamer who cherished his precious self-time more than anything else.

And so his days continued like this: a comfortable loop of university life, train rides, and returning to his sanctuary. Friends existed on the periphery, invitations were politely deflected when they threatened his peace, and his heart remained safely untouched by the confusing world of romance.

For now, at least, Eon was exactly where he wanted to be.

Weeks passed in this comfortable rhythm. The weather grew warmer as May approached. Eon finished his midterms with decent scores, celebrated quietly by buying a new figure of his current waifu—a elegant silver-haired sorceress from a lesser-known fantasy anime. He spent an entire Saturday carefully unboxing her, taking photos from every angle, and finding the perfect spot on his shelf.

One evening, after a particularly long gaming session, he stepped out onto his tiny balcony for some fresh air. The city lights of Tokyo stretched out endlessly. From his modest fourth-floor view, he could see the faint glow of Shinjuku in the distance, the towering skyscrapers, and the occasional train passing by like glowing snakes.

He leaned on the railing, sipping a cold ramune soda.

“Life’s pretty good,” he said softly to the night.

A gentle breeze ruffled his hair. Somewhere below, he heard laughter—probably a group of young people heading out for the night. For a brief moment, Eon wondered what it would be like to be part of that world. To have someone to share these nights with. To experience the things people wrote songs and anime about.

But the thought felt distant, almost like watching a different genre of show. His was a slice-of-life story about a contented solo protagonist. No need for sudden romance flags or dramatic confessions.

Eon finished his drink, crushed the can lightly, and went back inside.

He booted up Eternal Realms once more, the familiar login screen greeting him like an old friend.

“Another night, another adventure,” he whispered with a smile.

And just like that, Eon dove back into his world—where he was the hero, the weeb, the gamer, and perfectly, happily alone in his little Tokyo kingdom.

The story of his peaceful otaku life continued, day after comfortable day…

(And it could keep going like this for a very long time—unless, of course, Tokyo decided to throw an unexpected event his way one ordinary afternoon.)
