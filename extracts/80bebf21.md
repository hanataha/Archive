## Opening Grok

Eon adjusted his black-rimmed glasses and let out a long, satisfied sigh as he stepped into his small 1K apartment in the quiet residential area of Nakano, Tokyo. The door clicked shut behind him with a familiar, comforting sound. It was a Tuesday evening in late April, and the cherry blossoms outside had already begun to fade, their pink petals scattered across the narrow streets like forgotten confetti.

“Finally… home,” he muttered to himself, a small smile tugging at his lips.

He kicked off his sneakers at the genkan, not bothering to line them up perfectly—there was no one here to scold him anyway. His parents were back in Fukuoka, a good ten-hour shinkansen ride away. They called every Sunday like clockwork, asking if he was eating properly and if he’d made any “nice friends” at university. Eon always gave the same vague, reassuring answers: “Yeah, I’m fine. Classes are okay. Made some friends in the club.” He never mentioned how much he preferred coming back to this empty apartment over hanging out late with those friends.

The apartment was tiny by any standard—about 20 square meters—but to Eon, it was his personal kingdom. One room served as living room, bedroom, study, and dining area all at once. The walls were lined with cheap metal shelves he’d assembled himself from IKEA, now groaning under the weight of his treasures: rows upon rows of anime figures still in their boxes, a carefully curated manga collection organized by genre and release year, and several Gunpla models in various stages of completion. In the corner stood his pride and joy: a 27-inch curved monitor mounted on a sturdy desk, flanked by RGB keyboard and mouse that glowed softly in the dim light.

## Late Grok

Several months had passed since that rainy afternoon when Risa first spotted the Shield Hero clip on Eon’s phone. Summer had come and gone, bringing with it the sticky heat of Tokyo’s rainy season and then the crisp air of early autumn. The leaves in Nakano and around campus were beginning to turn shades of gold and red, and Eon’s small apartment now had a new stack of limited edition autumn-themed goods on his shelves.

Their relationship had deepened into something solid and comfortable—close friendship, the kind that felt natural even when they were doing nothing special. They texted almost every day, shared long voice calls late at night when both were procrastinating on assignments, and met up at least three or four times a week. Risa had become a regular presence in Eon’s life, and surprisingly, he didn’t mind the way his once perfectly isolated routine had been gently nudged to make space for her.
